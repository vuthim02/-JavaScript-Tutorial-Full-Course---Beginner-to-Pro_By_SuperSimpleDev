What to Learn at Each Level

1

Basic

HTML tags, CSS box model, colors, fonts, simple layout with divs. No design thinking yet.

2

Elementary

Flexbox, Grid basics, responsive with media queries, picking nice colors, removing default styles.

3

Intermediate

8px grid system, typography scale, color theory, card layouts, hover effects, basic Figma skills.

4

Advanced

Design systems, CSS animations, accessibility (WCAG), scroll effects, component architecture.

5

Excellent

Original design thinking, advanced motion, micro-interactions, performance optimization, leading projects.

6

Expert

Design strategy, team leadership, brand systems, user research integration, business impact.

7

Elite

Defines industry trends. Creates new patterns. Others study YOUR work. Shapes the future of design.

The Honest Answer

Stripe is not perfect. It's excellent for SaaS — clean, trustworthy, conversion-focused. But it plays it safe. It's "corporate beautiful."

Apple is not perfect. It's excellent for products — aspirational, emotional, premium. But it's repetitive. Every product page looks the same.

Craigslist is not bad. It's a deliberate choice — function over form. It works for its audience. But it teaches you what NOT to do for modern products.

The real answer: You need to understand ALL THREE to become excellent.
Study Stripe for structure. Study Craigslist for what to avoid. Study Apple for restraint and emotion.
